# Rust foundations

Start here if Rust is new to you. These eight small programs teach the language tools we will need before the viewer becomes large. Type each program, predict its assertions, and deliberately change it. Allow about **8–21 active study hours in total**. Each page gives its own rough range; take shorter sittings when useful.

The notebook is separate from your viewer project. You do not receive a completed viewer hidden behind these exercises. After the notebook, the cumulative course begins with empty source files.

- [1 · Names, numbers and functions](foundations/01-values.md) — about 1–2 hours
- [2 · Records and methods](foundations/02-records.md) — about 1–2 hours
- [3 · Arrays, vectors and slices](foundations/03-lists.md) — about 1–2 hours
- [4 · Ownership and borrowing](foundations/04-ownership.md) — about 1–3 hours
- [5 · Missing values and errors](foundations/05-errors.md) — about 1–3 hours
- [6 · Enums and state machines](foundations/06-states.md) — about 1–3 hours
- [7 · Traits, closures and shared ownership](foundations/07-behavior.md) — about 1–3 hours
- [8 · Bytes, alignment and shader contracts](foundations/08-bytes.md) — about 1–3 hours

An hour is a stopping point, not a deadline. If you cannot explain a borrow or predict a branch yet, spend another sitting on it. Knowing why a line works matters more than getting to the next page.
